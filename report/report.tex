\begin{abstract}
A scanned document page is mostly blank paper, yet an OCR-free document understanding model
encodes one as thousands of visual tokens that its decoder attends to at every generation
step. We insert a learned patch-saliency scoring head between a frozen hierarchical vision
encoder and an autoregressive text decoder, rank the encoder's 4800 patch tokens, and
discard the low-ranked ones before the decoder sees them. On full-page recognition over the
FUNSD test split of noisy scanned forms we report three results, each with its condition.
Discarding 65 percent of the visual tokens frees 65 percent of the decoder cross-attention
key-value cache, from 150.00 to 52.50 MiB, for a loss of 0.26 points of word recall on 50
paired documents, well inside noise; the same operation on a checkpoint that never trained
under pruning costs 6.03 points, so the result is a property of pruning-aware training
rather than of the pruner. The learned ranking beats a random mask by 17.7 to 34.0 points at
matched token budgets, the margin widening as the budget tightens. A difference-in-differences
design on bit-identical token sets rejects the competing explanation that pruning acts as
inference-time denoising. We also report what the architecture does not deliver: a fivefold
token cut yields only 1.04 times the wall-clock throughput, because both inserted stages sit
downstream of the frozen encoder, and a token-merging stage scored under an analysis protocol
fixed in advance returns underpowered rather than a null. That protocol retired a claim we
had previously reported.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A document page is mostly empty. Donut~\cite{donut} renders one at $2560 \times 1920$ and its
Swin-B~\cite{swin} encoder emits an $80 \times 60$ grid of \textbf{4800} patch tokens, every
one of which the mBART~\cite{mbart} decoder cross-attends to at every generation step.
Margin, inter-line whitespace and blank regions make up the majority of that grid. This
invites an obvious question --- how few tokens can be kept and still transcribe the page ---
and a less obvious one: does \emph{learning} which to keep beat not learning it?

We study the setting a document OCR model actually presents: the encoder is \emph{frozen} and
pretrained, the decoder is autoregressive over several hundred steps, and token reduction can
therefore only be applied at the encoder--decoder interface. That placement decides what the
method can and cannot buy, and most of this paper is about separating the two.

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item A learned patch-saliency router at the encoder--decoder interface that discards 65\% of
the visual tokens for a measured $-0.26$ recall points, with the accompanying
\emph{conditional} isolated by a dedicated ablation: the result requires pruning-aware
training and does not transfer to a model trained unpruned
(Sections~\ref{sec:budget} and~\ref{sec:why}).
\item A quantification of the learned ranking's value against a random mask at matched
budgets, $+17.7$ to $+34.0$ points, together with the negative result that it does not
significantly beat a hand-crafted ink heuristic (Section~\ref{sec:selection}).
\item A rejection, by difference-in-differences on bit-identical token sets, of the
inference-time-denoising explanation for why pruning helps (Section~\ref{sec:why}).
\item An honest efficiency accounting: the saving is decoder cross-attention memory and
\emph{not} latency or encoder compute, with the architectural reason measured rather than
asserted (Section~\ref{sec:efficiency}).
\item A pre-registered analysis protocol for the token-merging question, fixed in advance of
the deciding run, which returned \emph{underpowered} and retired a previously reported claim
of ours (Sections~\ref{sec:stats} and~\ref{sec:merge}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}

Token reduction for vision transformers is an active area. DynamicViT~\cite{dynamicvit}
prunes tokens with learned scores, and Token Merging~\cite{tome} combines redundant tokens
without any training at all. Both were developed for classification backbones, where
reduction applied early compounds through the remainder of the network and therefore buys
compute directly.

Document OCR breaks that assumption in two ways that are central to this paper. The encoder
is a frozen pretrained Swin transformer, so reduction cannot be inserted inside it without
retraining and without invalidating every earlier result; and the decoder runs for several
hundred autoregressive steps against an encoder that runs once, so the cost the reduction can
reach is decoder-side rather than encoder-side. The consequence is quantified in
Section~\ref{sec:efficiency} and is the reason this paper separates a memory claim from a
compute claim rather than reporting a single compression percentage.

\subsection{Saliency Router}
\label{sec:router}

The router is a two-layer MLP scoring head, $\mathrm{Linear}(1024 \rightarrow 256)
\rightarrow \mathrm{LayerNorm} \rightarrow \mathrm{GELU} \rightarrow
\mathrm{Linear}(256 \rightarrow 1) \rightarrow \sigma$, applied per token. Hard top-$k$
selection is made differentiable with a straight-through estimator~\cite{ste}, as in
\eqref{eq:ste}.

\begin{equation} \label{eq:ste}
M_{\text{ste}} = \mathrm{TopK}(s) + s - \mathrm{detach}(s)
\end{equation}

The forward pass therefore performs discrete filtering while gradients from the decoder's
cross-entropy reach the scorer. The head is 263{,}169 parameters, roughly $0.1\%$ of the
model.

An auxiliary supervision term on the scoring head is necessary rather than optional. Trained
on the task loss alone at a keep ratio of $1.0$, the straight-through multiplier is exactly
$1$ and the only gradient the scorer receives asks \emph{``does scaling this token's
magnitude help''} rather than \emph{``is this token informative''}. Those two objectives
anti-align: the router became an \textbf{inverted} saliency detector, ranking blank paper
above text. The diagnostic signature was that inverting its own scores at evaluation time
scored $73.09$ while the forward router scored $18.80$ --- strong signal, wrong sign. We
added a sign-correct supervision term on the head, after which the inverted mode collapsed
from $73.09$ to $2.70$, confirming the role flip rather than merely observing an improvement.

\subsection{Token Merging}

The merger is a training-free bipartite soft-merge in the style of Token
Merging~\cite{tome}, applied to the retained set. Membership of the two halves is computed
from each token's \emph{original} page position on a checkerboard of
$(\text{row} + \text{col}) \bmod 2$, which makes the partition invariant to the order in
which the router emits tokens. The stage has no parameters. Its evaluation is the subject of
Section~\ref{sec:merge} and its status is unresolved.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Experimental Design}

Every comparison reported here is \emph{paired per document} over the same evaluation set and
\emph{within a single checkpoint and compute environment}. Three design choices follow from
that and were fixed before any result was read.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The reference arm is always the same model.} A token budget is scored against
that checkpoint's \emph{own} unpruned row, never against a different checkpoint's, because
the unpruned ceiling moves between retrains (Section~\ref{sec:measurement}).
\item \textbf{Each intervention has a degenerate control at the same budget.} Learned
selection is scored against a random mask drawn at the identical token count, and the
pruning-aware checkpoint against one trained unpruned on \emph{bit-identical} token sets.
\item \textbf{Thresholds are fixed before the measurement they judge.} The minimum detectable
effect required of a null, the margin required of the router against the ink heuristic, and
the full analysis rule for the merging question (Section~\ref{sec:stats}) were all written
down in advance of the runs they score.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data, Task and Training}

Full-page OCR into a single \texttt{\{"text": ...\}} target. Training uses the FUNSD
train split~\cite{funsd} (149 images) plus 500 synthetic document pages; evaluation uses the
FUNSD test split, which is 50 images and is the entire split. Training ran on one free-tier
cloud T4 GPU with discriminative learning rates (router $10^{-4}$, decoder
$2 \times 10^{-5}$, unfrozen encoder tail $10^{-5}$). The evaluated checkpoint is trained
with pruning active at a keep ratio of $0.50$ for five epochs, resumed from a model trained
unpruned.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}

Held constant across every run reported here: FUNSD test $\times$ 50 images; word recall,
character accuracy, word order and mean normalised edit distance; \texttt{MAX\_WORDS} 128;
\texttt{max\_length} 512; task prompt \texttt{<s\_doc>}; \texttt{repetition\_penalty} $1.0$
with \texttt{no\_repeat\_ngram\_size} 3.

The decoding configuration is part of the protocol and not an incidental detail. An earlier
setting of \texttt{repetition\_penalty} $=1.3$ cost $27$ points of recall on this task, for a
mechanical reason worth stating: the penalty divides the logit of every already-emitted
token, and a full-page form legitimately reuses its entire vocabulary, so after roughly
eighty words almost every plausible continuation is downweighted while the end-of-sequence
token --- never emitted --- never is. The model was not transcribing the page incorrectly; it
was \emph{stopping}, at 71\% of gold length. The same root cause held valid-JSON output at
$0\%$.

\subsection{Two Compute Environments}
\label{sec:environments}

Training runs on a cloud T4; the diagnostics that produce every table below run locally on
CPU against cached checkpoints. The same weights on the same 50 images score $77.74$ in the
first environment and $74.72$ in the second --- a different device and a different library
version. Token \emph{selection} reproduces across the two to three decimal places;
\emph{generation} does not, and the gap widens as the budget tightens. \textbf{Every figure
in this paper is therefore a within-checkpoint, within-environment paired delta.} No number
here compares an absolute level measured in one environment to a level measured in the other.

\subsection{Statistical Analysis}
\label{sec:stats}

Each configuration is scored on all 50 test documents, and every reported delta is the mean
of the \emph{per-document} differences with a paired standard error over the same 50
documents; $t$ is that mean over its standard error. Deltas and $t$ values are computed from
the stored per-image arrays rather than from column means, so a reported figure can be
re-derived from the published artifact. The minimum detectable effect quoted for a null row
is twice its paired standard error.

For the token-merging question we fixed a fuller analysis rule in advance, because that
question is the one with an effect size comparable to the measurement noise. The rule names
the primary contrast and its control row before either is scored, and pins the estimator, the
standard error, the interval method, a tail statistic alongside the location statistic, the
multiplicity treatment over three confirmatory quantities, and a table stating what each
verdict licenses. The location estimator is a 10\% trimmed mean of per-document differences
with a Tukey--McLaughlin standard error~\cite{tukey}; intervals are deterministic at
$\pm 1.96$ standard errors rather than bootstrapped, since a bootstrap endpoint is one draw
rather than a value; multiplicity is Holm step-down~\cite{holm} at $\alpha = 0.05$. The
justification on record for each choice is the \emph{variance structure} of the paired
differences --- five of fifty documents carry three-quarters of the sum of squares --- and
never the sign of any contrast.

The rule is also the reason a location estimator alone was not considered sufficient. A
trimmed mean is exactly blind to the tail it discards: on the merging contrasts, pushing the
single worst document a further 25 points leaves the estimate unchanged to the last bit. An
accuracy-\emph{preservation} claim is a claim about precisely those documents, so the rule
names a tail quantity and its threshold in the same breath as the location one, and a
tail-gate failure vetoes the verdict that would license the word ``free''.

\subsection{Execution-Level Verification}
\label{sec:verification}

Each diagnostic re-derives its numbers from cached result artifacts, so any claim here is
recheckable on a CPU without a training run. Fourteen numbered diagnostics plus a memory
measurement are each a standalone, seeded script that prints an explicit verdict and a
concern list. Several falsified the hypothesis they were built to support: the first inverted
the plan it was meant to enable, the second overturned the first's recommendation in under a
second of compute, and the study in Section~\ref{sec:why} rejected the explanation this work
had carried for a week. The memory study's pre-written framing --- ``a large reduction in a
small quantity'' --- was falsified by its own output at $19.46\%$, and the script now derives
that sentence from the measurement instead of asserting it.

Every notebook edit goes through a patcher that backs up first, asserts each anchor matches
exactly once, and parses the result; every patcher has a companion that \emph{executes the
real cell} against stubs, with no GPU and no weight download. The training loop, the loss
block, the mid-run weight-swap control and the results writer are all covered this way. Three
correctness defects were found here rather than by a run failing: the decoding setting above,
the straight-through trap of Section~\ref{sec:router}, and a supervision term that fed a
probability to a loss expecting a logit, attenuating the gradient by up to $112\times$ on
exactly the tokens the scorer was most wrong about.

Passing checks receive the same scrutiny as failing ones. Before a green check is trusted we
ask what a \emph{failing} system would score on that same check; if the answer is ``about the
same'', the check is decorative and is rebuilt. The clearest case: a router probe printed ``no
concerns'' across five sections, and a sixth section comparing the router against the actual
objective reversed the verdict entirely --- the five clean sections were measuring properties
a broken router also satisfies. Controls are correspondingly chosen so that they can fail;
one acceptance gate was moved off a noisy recall delta onto a near-deterministic quantity,
and a mid-flight anchor was pinned to a number that did not yet exist and then reproduced it
at a gap of $0.00$.

Three properties distinguish this from an underpowered null.

\subsubsection{The experiment is powered}
A flat row is uninformative if the design could not have resolved a real effect. The paired
standard error puts the minimum detectable effect at \textbf{2.76} and \textbf{2.81} points
on the two free rows, against a threshold of $3.0$ fixed before the measurement. ``We looked
and saw nothing'' is therefore distinguishable from ``we could not have seen it,'' and this
is the former. The two tight budgets are reported as costs rather than omitted.

\subsubsection{The memory figure is measured twice by independent routes}
It is computed analytically as $2 \times \text{layers} \times \text{heads} \times
\text{head\_dim} \times \text{tokens} \times 4\,\text{B}$ and also captured from the
decoder's real \texttt{past\_key\_values} through a forward hook. The two agree to a relative
gap of $0.0000$ at all five budgets, with a control asserting that the captured tensor's
sequence length equals the number of compressed tokens the generation path reports.
Self-attention KV, which pruning does not touch, is separately asserted to be a function of
generated length only and is non-monotone in the keep ratio, so a self/cross mix-up could not
be mistaken for a pruning win. Twenty-five of twenty-five controls pass.

\subsubsection{The headline budget was fixed by a rule}
keep$=$0.20 would give a larger memory reduction ($-80.0\%$) and a better-sounding number; it
costs $13.01$ points. The measurement script pins the reported budget to the tightest one
that is free and fails a control if that budget turns out not to be, so the framing cannot
drift toward the largest available percentage.

\subsubsection{Scope}
Cross-attention KV only --- not total memory, not peak, not the encoder, and not latency.
Unpruned cross-attention KV is \textbf{19.46\%} of the model's 771\,MiB of parameters, so
this is a large reduction in a real quantity, but it is one quantity. Byte figures halve at
fp16 and scale with batch size; the percentage is invariant to both.

\subsection{Why Pruning Is Free: Train/Test Matching, Not Denoising}
\label{sec:why}

For a period this work recorded that pruning \emph{helped} at some budgets and that the
reason was unknown, with ``some regularisation-like effect'' as the standing guess. We wrote
down two hypotheses before testing them.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{H1, train/test matching.} The checkpoint trains at keep$=$0.50, so evaluating
it unpruned is off-distribution. Its unpruned ``ceiling'' is depressed, and the apparent gain
from pruning is a \emph{recovery} toward its own operating point.
\item \textbf{H2, inference-time denoising.} Most of a page is blank paper, which dilutes
cross-attention. If that is the mechanism, pruning should also help a model that never
trained with it.
\end{itemize}

A second checkpoint --- trained \emph{and} evaluated unpruned --- discriminates between them.
H2 predicts its curve improves under pruning; H1 predicts it peaks unpruned and falls. Both
checkpoints were evaluated with ink-based selection, which is weight-independent, so
\textbf{both see bit-identical token sets} (verified at a gap of $0.00 \times 10^{0}$ at all
four budgets). Every difference between the curves is therefore attributable to the weights
(Table~\ref{tab:did}).

\textbf{H2 is rejected.} The unpruned-trained model declines monotonically where H2 predicted
improvement, and the pruning-trained model peaks at exactly the budget it was trained for. H2
was moreover given its most favourable condition and still failed: the ink oracle retains
$0.994$ of the page's ink at keep$=$0.50, so what was discarded really was blank paper, which
is precisely what H2 says should help.

The deliverable therefore requires a conditional. Pruning to a third of the visual tokens
costs nothing \emph{if the model is trained for it}. It is not a property of the pruner.

\subsubsection{Isolating the confound}
The two checkpoints above differ by pruning-aware training \emph{and} by five more epochs of
it, so the comparison establishes that a difference exists rather than what caused it. We ran
the isolating arm: the same configuration with the training keep ratio moved
$0.50 \rightarrow 1.00$ and nothing else changed, verified by diffing the executed
notebooks --- the five epochs \emph{without} the pruning. Paired on $n = 50$: removing the
pruning while keeping the epochs costs $\mathrm{ISO}(0.35) = \bm{-10.49}$ points
($t = -3.74$); that run's budget curve peaks unpruned and declines monotonically where the
pruning-aware curve is flat; and five unpruned epochs moved the ceiling by $+0.52$ points
($t = 0.24$), so the epochs alone buy nothing. The gain is attributable to pruning-aware
training.

One limit remains, and it is why the wording is precise: the isolation holds at keep$=$0.35,
$0.25$ and $0.20$ ($-8.15$, $-7.35$, $-7.92$ locally, every $t$ at or beyond $-2.70$). The
\emph{loose} budgets are \textbf{underpowered, not null}. The defensible statement is that H1
is isolated at keep$=$0.35/0.25/0.20, and never that H1 is proven.

\subsection{Token Merging at a Matched Budget Returns Underpowered}
\label{sec:merge}

The merging stage's only defensible estimand is token-matched: at an equal budget, does
merging preserve more word recall than simply pruning harder? Scored under the rule of
Section~\ref{sec:stats}, at a matched budget of $M = 1920$ visual tokens, the pre-registered
primary contrast measures $\bm{+1.66}$ $[-0.81, +4.14]$ in one run and $\bm{+0.02}$
$[-1.90, +1.94]$ in the other, with \textbf{0 of 3} confirmatory quantities surviving Holm
correction in either. Both are flat, and both sit well above the 1.0-point resolution the
design targets, so the verdict is \textbf{underpowered}, which licenses nothing: not
``merging is free'', not ``no measured cost'', not even ``merging changes nothing''.

Reaching the target resolution requires $n \gtrsim 307$ documents. FUNSD's test split is 50
documents and that is the entire split, so $n$ was never a free parameter in this design. A
pooled evaluation that would have reached $n \approx 410$ was built and verified --- a
29-row sweep including a structure-matched random arm at $M = 1920$ that the completed runs
structurally could not supply, with cost measured at 5.92\,h against a 9\,h budget --- and
was not run, for the reason given in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

Applying the rule also exposed a defect in the rule. Its tail gate fires on 13 of 21
contrasts in which nothing is merged at all, so the gate is sensitive but not specific to the
mechanism it was written to police; the bar it needs is an active non-merge comparator at the
same budget, which is the structure-matched random arm named above.

\subsection{Efficiency Accounting: What Is Saved and What Is Not}
\label{sec:efficiency}

\subsubsection{No latency or throughput win, and the reason is structural}
Reducing 4800 visual tokens to 960 --- a $5\times$ reduction --- yields $\bm{1.04\times}$
wall-clock, with a maximum of $1.05\times$ across ten measured rows. Both the router and the
merger sit \emph{after} the frozen Swin encoder, so all 4800 tokens are computed at every
budget, and generation is decoder-bound at 243--280 autoregressive steps against an encoder
that runs once. Any latency or throughput claim for this architecture is false, and an
earlier framing of this work that leaned on one was retracted.

\subsubsection{No encoder-side saving, and the quantity is zero rather than small}
A per-stage analysis of the encoder's own configuration places the merger downstream of
\emph{all twenty} encoder blocks, so its encoder saving is $0\%$ by construction. There is no
cheap remedy: inserting it one stage earlier caps the saving at $4.9\%$ even at a 50\% merge
ratio, because only $9.7\%$ of the encoder lies downstream of that point. The version that
would pay must precede stage 2, which holds $69.2\%$ of the encoder in 14 of its 20 blocks,
inside a frozen pretrained encoder whose window partitioning, shifted-window cyclic shift and
relative position bias all assume an intact spatial grid.

\subsubsection{The efficiency claim is narrow by construction}
Of the $2.50\times$ cross-attention KV reduction available at $M = 1920$ with pruning and
merging combined, \textbf{pruning supplies $2.0\times$ and the merge step alone
$1.25\times$}, so the combined ratio is not a merging result and we do not quote it as one.

\subsection{Measurement Limits of the Design}
\label{sec:measurement}

Three further quantities were measured and bound how the results above may be read.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The unpruned ceiling moves between retrains} --- $1.09$ and $1.30$ points on
two runs, and $10.93$ on an earlier one where the drift cannot be separated from a harness
fault. Every comparison is therefore within-checkpoint, and each run from that point on
re-evaluates unchanged reference weights through the same harness first. The two early runs
remain ambiguous retroactively: no control can be added to a run that has already happened.
\item \textbf{Roughly 18\% of predictions are still not valid JSON.} Measured as a rate only
and never characterised; predictions are deliberately never repaired before scoring, so this
is a floor on the reported recall rather than a separate defect.
\item \textbf{The merger's ordering fix is correct about the partition and unproven about
recall.} Deriving the bipartite split from the original raster index rather than from score
rank takes missed vertical redundancy from $100\%$ to $0\%$, but the head-to-head against the
previous split is null on three checkpoints.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The result worth carrying away is a conditional, not a headline. A learned router at the
encoder--decoder interface of a document OCR model discards 65\% of the visual tokens and
65\% of the decoder's cross-attention KV cache for $-0.26$ points of word recall (paired
standard error $1.41$, $n = 50$), and its ranking is worth $+17.7$ to $+34.0$ points over a
random mask at matched budgets. The same operation on a checkpoint not trained for it costs
$6.03$ points. The distance between those two sentences is the finding: token pruning at this
interface is not a free inference-time transformation that can be applied to a trained model,
and a reader who takes only the first sentence will reach for it in exactly the setting where
it does not hold. The practical implication is that the pruning budget belongs in the
training recipe, not in the deployment configuration.

Where the architecture's placement is concerned, the negative results are the more
transferable ones. Because both inserted stages are downstream of a frozen encoder, the
method can only ever reduce decoder-side work, and the measurements say so: a fivefold token
cut buys $1.04\times$ wall-clock and $0\%$ of encoder compute
(Section~\ref{sec:efficiency}). Any future attempt to turn token reduction on a document
model into a speed result has to move the reduction inside the encoder, and we specify what
that costs --- a merge that preserves window partitioning, a defined cyclic shift and a
relative-position-bias rule for merged tokens, encoder retraining, and a fresh unpruned
ceiling --- rather than attempt it here. We therefore describe the merger as a decoder-side
memory mechanism and nothing more.

\subsection{Limitations}

The token-merging stage is unresolved and is reported as unresolved. Its matched-budget
contrast is flat in both runs but at a resolution several times coarser than the effect being
sought, and \emph{underpowered} is not a null (Section~\ref{sec:merge}). The pooled
evaluation that would have resolved it was built, verified and not run, for one reason: no
second corpus with licensing we could establish. The first-party-licensed candidate annotates
only a key-value subset of each page rather than the page itself, making its recall a
different quantity at a measurement grain $4.2\times$ coarser than the effect being resolved;
the candidate with the right annotations carried only an uploader-asserted licence with no
attribution to the competition that produced the scans, and the official distribution of that
corpus ships no licence, terms or citation file of any kind. We stopped at data we could not
account for rather than publish a number resting on it. This is the limitation we would
address first, and it is a data-access problem rather than a modelling one.

The remaining limitations are each measured rather than estimated, and are stated where they
bear on a result: $n = 50$ test images, which drives the $\approx 2.8$-point resolution on
the free rows and makes the loose-budget isolation arms underpowered rather than null;
generation that diverges between the two compute environments, widening as the budget
tightens, which is why every figure here is a paired within-environment delta rather than a
level; an unpruned ceiling that drifts between retrains; and a residual rate of malformed
structured output (Section~\ref{sec:measurement}). The learned ranking's advantage over a
hand-crafted ink heuristic is directional and below the threshold fixed in advance, and is
not claimed (Section~\ref{sec:selection}).

\subsection{On the Analysis Protocol}

We treat the measurement layer as part of the contribution, because several of the findings
above came from it rather than from additional training. The pre-registered rule of
Section~\ref{sec:stats} is the clearest case. The run it was written to score was never
collected, so it was instead applied \emph{backwards} to two completed runs --- and the
verdicts did not survive it. Two figures this work had been reporting as results turned out
to reproduce exactly in arithmetic while failing as \emph{verdicts}: one because its sign
flips with both the estimator and the replicate, the other because it is unreplicated across
the two runs. A pre-registration that only ever confirms is not doing any work; this one cost
us a headline, which is the evidence that it was real. The companion lesson, which
generalises past this project, is that an estimator chosen after seeing the effect is not a
measurement, and that a passing check deserves the same interrogation as a failing one
(Section~\ref{sec:verification}).

\section{Conclusion}

A learned router at the encoder--decoder interface of a document OCR model discards 65\% of
the visual tokens and 65\% of the decoder's cross-attention KV cache for $-0.26$ points of
word recall, and its ranking is worth $+17.7$ to $+34.0$ points over a random mask at matched
budgets. The same operation on a checkpoint not trained for it costs $6.03$ points; the
distance between those two sentences is the result, and it is why the claim carries a
conditional rather than a headline. What the architecture does not deliver is equally
measured: no latency win, no encoder saving, and a token-merging stage whose matched-budget
benefit remains underpowered at the only sample size this corpus admits.
